## The Value Stack (say all of this before revealing price)

When presenting a combo, list everything they're getting one by one. Make the list feel long. Then drop the price — it feels small against the stack.

### Example: Get Customers Stack
> "Here's everything you're getting:
> - A fully custom website — no templates, hand-coded, matches your brand
> - Mobile-optimized — looks perfect on every phone
> - All the copy written for you — you don't write a word
> - An ordering/booking form with live pricing
> - Every order automatically logged in Google Sheets — your free CRM
> - Instant email notification every time someone reaches out
> - On-page SEO — title tags, meta descriptions, Google-ready from day one
> - Google Business Profile setup
> - Your own custom domain, fully connected
> - Hosted free — no monthly platform fees
> - 1 month of free changes after launch
> 
> All of that — $750."

### The Formula
1. List every deliverable (make it 8+ items)
2. Pause
3. Say the price
4. Immediately follow with the ROI math

## Objection Responses

### "How much does it cost?"
> Don't answer yet. Stack first.
> "Let me show you everything you're getting first — then we'll talk numbers. I want you to see the full picture."

### "I already have a website"
> "Can I see it? Most sites I look at weren't built to bring in customers — they were built to exist. Let me show you what's different."
> Then pull up their site and point out specific issues (no form, no SEO, slow load, not mobile-friendly).

### "I don't have the budget right now"
> "That's exactly why we don't ask for anything upfront. I build the whole thing first — you see it, you use it, you decide. If you don't love it, you don't pay. Zero risk."

### "I need to think about it"
> "Totally fair. What specifically are you thinking about? I'd rather answer it now than have you sitting on a question."
> If they push back: "No pressure at all. I'll send you the preview link when it's ready and you can take your time."

### "That's too expensive"
> Go back to the ROI math. Make it specific to their business.
> "I get it. Let me put it this way — [ROI math for their niche]. The site pays for itself in [X months]. After that, every customer it brings in is pure profit."

### "I can build one myself on Wix/Squarespace"
> "You absolutely can. The question is — do you want to spend 20 hours figuring out a website builder, or do you want someone to hand you a finished site in 5 days that's already built to bring in customers? Plus there's no monthly platform fee with us."

### "Can I get a discount?"
> Don't discount the price — add value.
> "I don't do discounts, but I can add an extra month of free changes after launch. That way you get more time to dial everything in."

## Closing

### The Close Line
> "The only question is whether it makes sense to get started. When are you free this week to send over a few photos and your logo?"

### After They Say Yes
1. Confirm which combo/services they want
2. Ask for: logo, 5–10 photos, any specific text they want on the site, competitor sites they like
3. Set expectation: "First draft in 2 days. You'll get a preview link to look at on your phone and computer."
4. Send intake form or collect info right on the call

### After They Say No / Not Now
> "No worries at all. I'll keep your info on file — when you're ready, just message me and we'll pick up where we left off."
> Add them to follow-up list. Check in 2 weeks.

## The Golden Rules

1. **Stack before price.** Never say the number before listing everything they get.
2. **ROI before objection.** If they hesitate, do the math before they object.
3. **Risk reversal is your weapon.** "Don't love it — don't pay" ends 80% of hesitation.
4. **Ask, don't pitch.** Let them tell you what they need. Then show them you already built the answer.
5. **One person, start to finish.** They're not talking to a salesperson — they're talking to the person who builds their site. That's the advantage.
