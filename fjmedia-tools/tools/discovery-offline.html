Customer & Money

Where customers come from today and what they're actually buying. The answers here decide whether this build is a lead-gen engine or a credibility check — two very different sites.

If they ramble, ask them to retry in 15 words or less. Becomes the hero subhead seed.
IG / GBP / referral / Google / walk-in / paid ads. If 90% referral, this site is a trust check — not a lead engine.
e.g. "peace of mind", "a memorable celebration", "confidence walking into a room". This becomes the value-prop copy.
Drives local SEO targeting, GBP service-area setup, and footer content. "All of Winnipeg" or "Transcona + East Kildonan + Saint Boniface".
Brands they aspire to, neighborhoods they live in, the decade their taste sits in. e.g. "early-30s, drives a Tesla, shops Aritzia / Frank & Oak, follows Kinfolk." This locks photography tier + design polish more than age/income ever does.
Section 2 of 9 · Fuels: information hierarchy, primary CTA, secondary CTAs, feature priority

The Win & the CTA

Lock the one action every visitor must take — and the context they're arriving in — before deciding what's on the page.

Closer: they've already heard of you (referral, DM, business card), site needs to seal the deal → testimonials + guarantees + clear pricing FIRST. Door-opener: first-time discovery (Google, IG explore), site needs to hook them → benefits + transformation + visual proof FIRST. This single answer reorders the entire homepage.
Book / order / inquire / call / DM / get a quote. Pick one — this is the global CTA and it appears in nav, hero, and footer.
Call, DM, SMS, email, contact form, directions. Drives the secondary CTA bar + sticky-nav buttons.
Push for specifics: "can't book online", "no pricing visible", "no mobile site", "no reviews shown". Drives feature priority.

Services & Pricing

Every service the site needs to sell, in priority order, with the exact pricing presentation we'll use.

This is the raw input for the services section. Push for completeness now or risk going back twice.
Hierarchy decision — top 3 get featured cards, the rest get listed. Cash cow goes first.
Show exact prices · show "from $X" · show packages only · hide pricing (inquire). Drives the pricing block layout.
Becomes the FAQ section content + targets SEO long-tail keywords ("how much does X cost in Winnipeg", "do you serve Y").
Section 4 of 9 · Fuels: proof section, photography brief, copy scope, design system

Proof & Brand Assets

Audit what already exists vs. what we need to create. This is where scope balloons or stays tight.

Google, IG DMs, screenshots, Facebook, email, none yet. Drives testimonial section vs. "request after launch" plan.
Drives gallery feasibility. If nothing exists, the site can't lean on visual proof — needs to lean on social proof or guarantees.
Logo state (vector / PNG / needs refresh / nothing). Photos available (pro shoot already done / phone photos / nothing). Style preference: custom on-brand (book a shoot) · high-end stock (Pexels / Unsplash / paid stock) · authentic raw (on-the-job phone shots, lifestyle). Locks the entire image direction.
Hex codes if they have them. Otherwise I pick per the niche playbook. Drives the entire design system.
Biggest scope multiplier of the whole project. If I'm writing all copy, add 30–50% to the timeline + price tier.
The Work: projects, before-afters, finished results — portfolio-first sites (renovators, designers, photographers). The People: you + team — trust/relationship-first sites (realtors, therapists, salons, consultants). Locks portrait vs. project imagery balance + about-page weight.
Section 5 of 9 · Fuels: design system, layout archetype, motion level, anti-patterns

Look & Feel

The fastest path to design alignment is references and opposites. Don't accept "modern" or "clean" as answers — push to specifics every time.

Most operational question in the whole worksheet. Locks the layout archetype + motion level + density.
Equally critical. Sets the anti-pattern guardrails.
Push for textures, materials, eras, places: industrial · organic · handmade · clinical · editorial · gallery · workshop · velvet · concrete · linen · neon · brass. Vibe words like "modern, clean, professional" tell me nothing — material words lock the entire mood board.
Dark / light · Photo-driven / type-driven · Quiet / loud · Spacious / dense · Sharp / rounded · Static / scroll-animated. Each binary instantly narrows the design direction.
Not adjectives — specific things to ban: "no popups · no auto-playing video · no carousels · no countdown timers · no chat-bot bubble · no 'we' marketing speak · no stock photos of fake handshakes · no fade-in-on-scroll everywhere." Saves me from building something they'll ask me to rip out.
Section 6 of 9 · Fuels: "why us" section, meta description, value-prop copy

Competitive Edge

If their site looks like every competitor's, it earns nothing. Find the gap and build the messaging around it.

I'll audit what they do, what they show, what they charge. Drives positioning + features-they-don't-have analysis.
Price · speed · quality · story · certification · location · relationships. Becomes the "Why Us" section + meta description.
Not "I don't like their logo" — push to a UI / copy / tone specific: "their hero is just a stock photo of a handshake", "they list 47 services on one page", "their photos look like a 2014 dental brochure", "they sound corporate / desperate / cold". The opposite of what they hate becomes our hero subhead + visual hook.
